ABSTRACT

Soft continuum robotic arms o"er unparalleled dexterity and compliance. However, their virtually in#nite degrees of freedom
and nonlinear deformations pose signi#cant challenges for sensing and control. This paper proposes a vision-based framework
for distributed state estimation and closed-loop control of tendon-driven soft robotic arms. Miniature cameras embedded in each
section provide dense proprioceptive feedback, while convolutional neural networks map multi-view images to both tip pose and
full-body reconstruction using a polynomial curvature model. Two alternative controllers are designed and evaluated. The #rst is a
model-based null-space velocity controller that regulates tip pose while shaping tendon con#gurations. The second is a data-driven
inverse kinematics neural network that maps task-space goals to tendon commands, with explicit secondary-objective optimization during training. Both approaches are validated in open- and closed-loop settings on single- and multi-section manipulators
in quasi-static regimes. Experiments demonstrate that embedded vision enables accurate full-body state reconstruction, reliable
redundancy resolution, and robust regulation even under external disturbances.

Introduction

Soft robots draw inspiration from natural organisms to achieve
compliance and distributed motion [1]. They are designed to passively deform and recon#gure to navigate complex environments
and adapt to their surroundings. This embodied intelligence facilitates compliant coupling with external objects, stemming from
variable impedance characteristics [2] and potentially in#nite degrees of freedom. Among soft robots, continuum manipulators
are characterized by their slender con#guration, and capacity
to have complex 3D curvatures. Due to their adaptability and
safe interaction, they show promise in applications such as surgical tools for minimally invasive diagnosis [3], dexterous grasping and manipulation [4], and environmental monitoring and
exploration [5]. However, they still lack precision and robustness to external disturbances [6], requiring dense sensing and
redundancy-aware control.
Accurate proprioceptive control in soft arms depends on adequate feedback, as they are strongly a"ected by deformations

induced by environmental interactions, self-weight, and approximate modeling [7, 8]. Sensing technologies face major challenges,
both in embedding sensors throughout the body and in reconstructing the end-e"ector pose from indirect measurements. One
approach is to directly detect skin deformations using resistive or
piezoresistive strain and pressure sensors, or Fiber Bragg Grating
(FBG) nodes [5]. Another solution is to integrate Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) into the arm, enabling high-frequency
pose reconstruction while avoiding the occlusion and calibration
issues of external sensing [6]. IMUs also allow estimation of body
deformation under external forces [9].
However, IMU sensing su"ers from drift [10] and, being nondistributed, cannot capture the global strain state. Camera-based
approaches are increasingly used in soft robotics for pose reconstruction, as they can capture both global deformations and local
interactions [11], while avoiding the integration challenges of embedded strain or pressure sensors [5]. External setups have been
proposed, including stereometric systems for supervised shape
learning [12] and model-free approaches for closed-loop full-body
control of soft robots [7]. These methods nevertheless become

For proprioceptive performance, the sensing system should track
the robot motion from internal cameras, without interfering with
movement or compliance of the structure. Previous works have
embedded cameras inside the hollow body, often employing internal illumination such as structured light [13] to ensure consistent
imaging. In this framework, a common approach is to place visual
markers beneath the robot skin. To further improve robustness,
image binarization is often applied before inference [14], and simulation is used to pre-train models for zero-shot transfer to the
real world [15]. Approaches for reconstruction include geometric
optimization from images [16] and deep learning architectures
for state mapping. Embedded cameras provide high-resolution
and accurate measurements of global deformations, without requiring complex distributed sensing hardware. Due to these advantages, vision-based strategies have been exploited for contact
and displacement detection [11], tactile servoing [17], and force
estimation [18].
Onboard state estimation of soft structures allows for the deployment of various control architectures. Yet, controlling continuum
arms presents signi#cant challenges. These systems are inherently redundant [1] and therefore o"er multiple possibilities to
con#gure their actuation space. They possess extra degrees of
freedom beyond those required to accomplish the primary task,
a property referred to as kinematic redundancy in the null space.
This redundancy enables the optimization of secondary objectives, such as minimizing control e"ort, improving manipulability, and avoiding obstacles or singularities [19]. Prior works
have applied null-space control of soft robots, employing path
planning algorithms such as RRT [20] to randomize the robot
con#guration while reaching a goal, or teleoperating the tip of
a continuum manipulator with a rigid-link master to maximize
manipulability [21]. More advanced objectives, such as wholebody grasping [22], have been demonstrated by assigning and
regulating task-space coordinates at several locations along the
backbone. However, validation of these approaches has been
limited to simulation. Alternatively, researchers have controlled
tendon-driven origami soft robots by implicitly solving redundant
inverse kinematics through constrained gradient-based optimization, using motion capture for feedback [23].
Control strategies for continuum manipulators can be divided
into model-based and data-driven approaches [1]. Many modelbased controllers adopt a spatial backbone representation that
neglects volumetric deformations and focuses exclusively on the
behavior of the central axis [24]. A general kinematic formulation
is the Piecewise Constant Curvature (PCC) model [25], which
approximates the robot as a sequence of circular arcs. Variants
of this model have also been developed for Jacobian-based control [6]. More expressive alternatives include Cosserat rod models,
which capture shear and torsional deformations through a geometrically exact formulation [26]. FEM-based frameworks further leverage continuum mechanics for simulation and control,
at the cost of higher computational demand [27]. Data-driven
controllers address the limitations of these approaches, including
non-idealities in soft robotics such as friction, material anisotropy,
and cable slack [1]. One direction is adaptive control, where
an online learning loop updates model parameters from sensor
feedback [28]. Another is iterative learning control, where the

robot improves its behavior by incorporating errors from previous executions into the feedforward command while preserving
its natural compliance [2]. Alternatively, machine learning approaches can approximate the entire robot model. Researchers
have developed a biLSTM-based framework for planning and
control that relies only on motor encoder feedback [10].
Addressing the sensing and control challenges of continuum
manipulators, this paper proposes a strategy for vision-based reconstruction and control of soft robotic arms that scales with the
number of segments. Information from miniature cameras embedded in the sections is fused within a single CNN to achieve
full-body reconstruction. The reconstructed state is then used as
feedback in both model-based and data-driven controllers, enabling tip regulation while reducing actuation e"ort. To the best
of our knowledge, no framework has yet combined such sensing
with closed-loop null-space control of continuum robots, either
through model-based or learning-based approaches.
The contributions of this paper include:
1. A distributed and scalable vision-based sensing strategy for
soft arms that uses miniature cameras embedded in each
section of the robot. It provides dense, markerless feedback
for full-body state reconstruction, and scales to arms with
di"erent numbers of sections through modular hardware
design and a lightweight CNN architecture.
2. Integration of embedded vision with model-based nullspace control. We close the loop by combining camera-based
state estimation with a Jacobian-based Piecewise Constant
Curvature velocity controller.
3. A data-driven framework for both sensing and control. We
train learning-based inverse kinematics models to generate
actuation commands while optimizing redundancy objectives.
4. Experimental validation of precise pose regulation using
both model-based and data-driven controllers, and demonstration of the advantages of closed-loop control under external disturbances.
In the remainder of this paper, Section 2 describes the robot setup,

presents the deep learning methods and the control architectures.
Section 4 outlines the experimental results. Section 5 summarizes
the paper and discusses potential directions for future work. A formal de#nition of all notation and terminology used throughout
these sections is provided in the Supporting Information.

Network Training

All networks were trained on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090
GPU. We used transfer learning within the same robot and across
the two manipulators: 9,1 networks were initialized with the 9.1

3.3

Controller Design

For the single-section robot, a controller is implemented to reach
a desired position, while the pose is controlled for multi-section
case. The multi-section arm is an overactuated system with nine
distributed actuators and six control degrees of freedom. An
example of the corresponding 3-DOF null space is shown in
Fig. 4b demonstrating how di"erent structural con#gurations,
whether compressed or bent, yield the same tip position and orientation. The manipulator can be shaped within its kinematics to
exploit these equivalent states.
All the designed kinematic controllers follow the same general
scheme shown in Fig. 4c. A trajectory planner ∳ 𝐵 (where 𝐵 is the
number of sections) generates a time-varying reference pose or
position. The error between the reference and the current state,
estimated in closed-loop by the CNN from real-time camera input,
is then fed to an inverse kinematics (IK) solver. This updates the
tendon actuations over time, driving the robot through its actual
forward kinematics (FK), until the distance to the goal falls below
a given threshold 𝐶.

Experimental Results

We evaluate the proposed framework on both single- and multisection soft manipulators. First, we evaluate tip pose estimation and curvature reconstruction from embedded cameras with
CNNs, considering quadratic, a$ne, and constant curvature
models. We further assess the data-driven kinematics in comparison to the PCC approximation. Then, we benchmark the proposed model-based and data-driven controllers on target reaching and trajectory tracking, and demonstrate the advantages of
closed-loop control under external disturbances.

5

Conclusion and Discussion

This work proposes a scalable framework for vision-based proprioception and control of soft continuum robotic arms. Miniature
cameras embedded in each segment feed deep learning algorithms that reconstruct the full shape of the robot, enabling
closed-loop control strategies, both model-based and data-driven,
which explicitly exploit kinematic redundancy to accomplish
secondary tasks within the null space.
Utilizing two tendon-driven continuum manipulators, we propose a minimally invasive and compliance-preserving design using a !exible opaque covering that scales along the arm length
without a"ecting its behavior. Experimental results demonstrate
that our sensing approach reaches competitive performance in
reconstructing both tip pose and full-body curvature, with preserved accuracy at workspace boundaries. Closed-loop control
achieves near-sensing performance and outperforms open-loop
alternatives, while being robust to external disturbances.
This sensing and control framework endows soft robots with accurate motion capabilities. Leveraging their redundancy, continuum manipulators can accomplish complex tasks across multiple
domains. For instance, they can be employed in search-andrescue or inspection within con#ned and cluttered environments,
or in minimally invasive surgery. In such settings, full-body curvature feedback from embedded cameras enables null-space control
to actively shape the arm for obstacle avoidance while maintaining the desired tip pose.
This strategy has some limitations, which may be addressed in
future work. At the sensing level, the embedded vision system
currently relies on ambient lighting provided by the external
environment. Illumination passes through the opaque covering,

and variability is mitigated through image augmentations during
training. To enable operation in poorly lit or nocturnal environments, the system should be extended with miniature internal
light sources. This paper primarily investigates vision alone, integrating encoder signals for Jacobian estimation. Unlike external
cameras, the internal setup avoids occlusion and #xed-placement
constraints, at the cost of lower update frequency. The 8 Hz update rate limits the framework to quasi-static operation, suitable
for applications such as inspection or surgical guidance; higher
rates could be achieved by fusing camera estimates with encoder
feedback through temporal #ltering. Future work should integrate the camera-based solution with alternative sensing strategies to improve con#guration accuracy. Moreover, the system
does not detect contact or force information, although cameras
could provide both. Extending proprioception to capture surface
interactions through training or simulation would enhance robot
capability, while force detection would add a feedback channel
for closed-loop control in dynamic conditions. On the control
side, future work will extend null-space control to additional secondary objectives. In this paper, redundancy was exploited to
balance tendon e"ort, but the same methodology applies to shaping arm con#gurations for obstacle avoidance while tracking a
desired end-e"ector trajectory. Furthermore, null-space control
could be leveraged to wrap around objects and enable grasping,
using curvature feedback to regulate how the robot conforms to
complex geometries.
