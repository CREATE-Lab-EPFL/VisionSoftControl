    Comment 1: Role of visual sensing in closed-loop control
    Reviewer's point: Unclear whether curvature feedback enters control; Algorithm 1's PCC-based tendon-length
    initialization appears unused once the loop starts.

    Response / substance:
    Checking Algorithms 1 and 2 against the text confirms the reviewer's reading exactly. In Algorithm 1, camera
    feedback fp3(I) supplies only the current pose p used in the task-space error (pref - p); the Jacobian J and the
    null-space term N·(l■ - l) are both computed from tendon lengths l read from the motor encoders, not from
    vision. Algorithm 2 (data-driven) uses only camera pose, with no encoder input at all, consistent with the text's
    claim that it "avoids the use of encoders." The curvature networks fC1 and fC3 never appear in either
    algorithm; they are evaluated only as a separate reconstruction capability (Sections 4.1.2 and 4.2.2). This
    three-way separation (camera pose for the task error, encoders for the Jacobian/null-space, curvature
    reconstruction as a standalone output) is correct in the implementation but not stated anywhere as a single,
    explicit sentence, which is what caused the ambiguity. On the flagged Algorithm 1 line: it computes an initial
    tendon-length estimate from the camera-derived tip positions via the PCC inverse map (Eq. 2) before the loop
    begins, but the very first loop iteration immediately overwrites l with an encoder reading, so this estimate
    never reaches J, N, or δl. Its only plausible remaining function is a pre-motion consistency check between the
    camera- and encoder-based state estimates (e.g., to catch a gross camera miscalibration before moving the
    motors) — but the manuscript does not currently claim this. This needs a decision.
    Manuscript changes:
●      Abstract: add one           clause distinguishing pose-based control feedback from curvature-based
       reconstruction.
●       Introduction, contribution items 2-3: state explicitly which signal (camera pose vs. encoder tendon length
        vs. curvature) feeds which part of the control law.
●       Fig. 4c caption: annotate that camera feedback enters only the pose/error path; encoders feed the
        Jacobian and null-space term.
●       Algorithm 1: either remove the flagged pre-loop line, or keep it with one sentence describing it as a
        pre-motion consistency check (see author input below).
●       Conclusion (Section 5): restate the same three-way distinction.
    Needs author input (not derivable from the manuscript alone): Was the camera-vs-encoder pre-loop comparison in
    Algorithm 1 actually monitored/used as a safety check during your experiments (e.g., logged and inspected, or used to
    abort a run)? If yes, keep the line and we can justify it with that evidence; if it was just a leftover from an earlier version
    of the algorithm, the cleanest fix is to delete it.


    Comment 2: Originality relative to prior work
    Reviewer's point: Individual sensing/control elements are not novel in isolation; originality must be established
    through direct comparison with named prior systems.

    Response / substance:
    Using the reviewer's own one-line descriptions of the five suggested papers (Yoo et al. is already cited as Ref.
    [15] in the current manuscript, for zero-shot sim-to-real internal-camera proprioception), the distinguishing
    angle for this paper is consistent across all five comparisons: this is, to our knowledge, the only system
    combining (i) distributed markerless internal cameras across a modular multi-section body, with (ii) both a
    model-based and a learned controller that explicitly exploit kinematic redundancy through a null-space
    secondary objective, validated on both single- and multi-section hardware. Hofer et al. (2021) reaches higher
    single-actuator accuracy (~1° at 30 Hz vs. our 3.11°±1.96° at 8 Hz) but on a single spherical actuator with no
    redundancy to exploit. Thuruthel et al. (2017) is the closest in spirit to our data-driven controller (learned
    inverse kinematics with feedback on a redundant tendon-driven arm, tested under load) — the distinguishing
    claim here would be the vision-based, encoder-free sensing pipeline and the explicit redundancy-weighted
    training loss (Eq. 11), but this must be checked against their actual feedback modality before writing it into the
    paper. Han et al. (2025) also uses an internal camera for full-body deformation and force estimation; the
    distinguishing claim is our closed-loop null-space control (which requires deciding whether their
    internal-camera system is used for control at all, or sensing only), and conversely their force-sensing
    capability is something we explicitly lack and already list as a limitation (Section 5). Lu et al. (2024) is
    FBG-based, not vision-based, and belongs in the related-work sensing discussion (Section 1, paragraph 2)
    rather than as a direct comparator. Yoo et al. targets sim-to-real transfer for a different actuation type; the
    distinguishing point is that our networks are trained on real, directly-collected data and are paired with
    closed-loop null-space control, which their work does not address.
    Manuscript changes:
●      Add a dedicated comparison paragraph in the Introduction, after the sentence ending "...closed-loop
       null-space control of continuum robots, either through model-based or learning-based approaches."
●       Sharpen the contribution list (Section 1, items 1-4) to state the integration claim precisely: distributed
        markerless internal-camera sensing + redundancy-aware control (model-based and learned) on a
        multisection tendon-driven arm.
    Needs author input (not derivable from the manuscript alone): This comparison is currently built only from the
    reviewer's one-sentence summaries. Each of the five papers should be read in full before this paragraph is finalized, in
    particular to confirm: Thuruthel et al.'s actual sensing modality (vision, motion capture, or embedded sensors), and
    whether Han et al. or Hofer et al. include any redundancy/null-space control. An inaccurate comparison is worse than
    none.


    Comment 3: Scalability claim overstated
    Reviewer's point: The estimator is not invariant to section count; the output head changes (6→18 pose variables,
    1→3 curvature parameter sets), not only the first convolutional layer, and only n=1/n=3 were tested.

    Response / substance:
    This is a direct factual correction to accept. Section 3.1 currently states that scaling to a different section count
    requires "adjusting only the first layer," but Fig. 3a and the text's own description of the regression head (6
    outputs for single-section, 18 for multi-section) confirm the output head also changes. The claim should be
    narrowed to what is actually demonstrated: a modular, transfer-learning-based architecture where the
    pretrained MobileNetV2 backbone is reused across section counts, with both the first convolutional layer
    (input channels) and the final regression head (output width) adapted and retrained for each new n. This has
    been validated only at n=1 and n=3; n=2 and n>3 were neither trained nor tested, and no claim of zero-shot
    generalization to an untested section count should remain.
    Manuscript changes:
●      Section 1, contribution item 1: rewrite "scales to arms with different numbers of sections" to state
       retraining is required, with the backbone reused via transfer learning.
●       Section 3.1, sentence describing Fig. 3a: correct "requires adjusting only the first layer" to include the
        output head.
●       Add one explicit sentence noting n=1 and n=3 are the only validated configurations.

    Comment 5: Curvature-coordinate inconsistency (normalized vs. dimensional arc
    length)
    Reviewer's point: Main-text Eq. 4 uses normalized arc length s ∈ [0,1]; SI Eqs. S5-S9 evaluate the polynomial at
    L/3, 2L/3, L and use c0 = θ/L, implying dimensional arc length. These cannot both be correct as written.

    Response / substance:
    This is a genuine, fixable inconsistency; it does not require new data, only consistent rederivation. The SI
    equations and the closed-form initialization already operate in dimensional arc length x ∈ [0, L] (using c0 =
    θ/L, which only has correct units if the independent variable is dimensional length), so the cleanest fix is to
    convert the main-text formulation to match the SI rather than the reverse, since that preserves the existing
    fitting code and initialization equations unchanged.
    Manuscript changes:
●      Eq. 4: rewrite as θ(x) = c0·x + (1/2)c1·x² + (1/3)c2·x³, x ∈ [0, L], with c0, c1, c2 now carrying units of
       curvature and its derivatives (1/length, 1/length², 1/length³), rather than the dimensionless coefficients
       implied by normalized s.
●       Eqs. 5a-5b: replace the normalized-s integration variable with x directly; remove the explicit extra factor of

●       Eq. 6 (least-squares fit): the evaluation points s = 1/3, 2/3, 1 become x = L/3, 2L/3, L, which then matches

    Comment 6: Secondary objective not quantitatively evaluated
    Reviewer's point: No experiment isolates the effect of the null-space secondary objective H(l); no reported
    tendon-limit margins, tendon distributions, or with/without comparison; H(l) penalizes deviation from mean tendon
    length, not force/current/energy directly.

    Response / substance:
    Two levels of response are possible here without new hardware trials. First, a retrospective quantitative
    report: tendon lengths were logged via motor encoders at every control iteration in both the target-reaching
    (Sections 4.1.4, 4.2.4) and trajectory-tracking (Sections 4.1.5, 4.2.5) experiments already run. H(l) (or
    equivalently the RMS deviation of each tendon from l■) can be computed directly from those logs for both
    controllers at convergence on all 20 k-medoids targets and along the tracked trajectories, giving exactly the
    "tendon-limit margins, tendon distributions" the reviewer asks for, as a re-analysis rather than a new
    experiment. Second, a genuine with/without ablation: using the already-trained forward-kinematics network
    Np3 (data-driven case) or the PCC forward map (model-based case), the resolved-motion-rate law (Eq. 8/10)
    can be re-run offline with the null-space term N·(l■-l) set to zero, driving toward the same 20 targets, and the
    resulting tendon distributions compared against the logged real runs with the secondary objective active. This
    isolates the objective's effect computationally; it should be labeled clearly as an offline/simulated comparison,
    not a new hardware ablation. On framing: H(l) is a quadratic penalty on deviation from the mean sampled
    tendon vector, which is correctly described as actuator-range centering; it does not directly minimize force,
    current, or energy unless those were separately measured.
    Manuscript changes:
●      Add a new figure/table (Section 4.1.4/4.2.4 or SI) reporting achieved H(l) / tendon-margin distributions
       from existing closed-loop logs, for both controllers.
●       If the offline zero-null-space simulation is run, add it as a companion panel with an explicit "simulated, not
        measured on hardware" label.
●       Revise the Eq. 9 description to state plainly that H(l) is an actuator-range-centering objective, and remove
        or qualify any language implying force/energy minimization unless motor current data substantiates it.
    Needs author input (not derivable from the manuscript alone): Is motor current/torque telemetry logged from the
    DYNAMIXEL XL330-M288-T motors during the closed-loop runs? If so, an energy-proxy claim becomes possible
    instead of only actuator-range centering; if not, the actuator-range-centering framing is the honest one to keep.
