                               Major comments


                               1. Clarify the role of visual sensing in closed-loop control
                               The manuscript presents pose estimation and full-body piecewise-
                               polynomial-curvature (PPC) reconstruction as parts of a unified
                               sensing-and-control framework. However, Algorithms 1 and 2 and
                               Fig. S3 indicate that both controllers use feedback from the pose
                               network, while the curvature network is evaluated separately and
                               does not enter either control law.
                               Algorithm 1 also uses a PCC inverse calculation to initialize tendon
                               lengths from section-tip positions. This is not the same as using the
                               image-based PPC curvature estimate. Moreover, the resulting
                               estimate appears to be replaced by the motor-encoder measurement
                               at the first control iteration, before it affects the control command.
                               The authors should explain this initialization or remove it.
                               The abstract, contribution list, Fig. 4c, and conclusion should clearly
                               distinguish:
                               camera-based pose estimation used for control;
                               encoder measurements used to calculate the Jacobian and null-
                               space term; and
                               image-based PPC reconstruction evaluated as a separate sensing
                               capability.
                               If the curvature estimate is intended to contribute to control, the
                               manuscript should explicitly show where it enters the control law.


                               2. Establish originality claims through direct comparison with prior

                               3. Substantiate or narrow the scalability claim
                               The estimator is not invariant to the number of arm sections. Images
                               from ncameras are stacked as ninput channels, so the first
                               convolutional layer changes with n. The output head must also
                               change—from 6 to 18 pose variables and from one to three sets of
                               curvature parameters. Separate networks are trained for the one-
                               and three-section arms.
                               Transfer learning may reduce retraining effort, but the present
                               method is not plug-and-play across arbitrary arm lengths and is not
                               validated on an unseen section count. The statement that only the
                               first layer must be adjusted also conflicts with Fig. 3 because the
                               regression head changes as well.

                               and that a learned network, N_C, maps tip pose to curvature labels.
                               Thus, the multi-section CNN is evaluated primarily against a learned,

                               backbone.
                               Fig. S2b does not provide sufficient validation of N_C: numerical
                               errors, sample count, data split, and confidence intervals are missing.

                               7. Expand and better characterize the control evaluation
                               The experimental evaluation does not quantify its coverage relative to
                               the reachable workspace. The training datasets reportedly include
                               bending angles up to 85^∘per section, and the multisection target
                               plots span a substantial positional range. However The trajectory
                               evaluation is narrow, and the target coverage is incompletely
                               documented. Single-section bending angles are apparently under 17
                               deg, and three-section angles are under 19 deg, and overall length
                               changes are also quite limited. The evaluation does not include
                               workspace-boundary paths, nonplanar trajectories, large orientation
                               changes, or trajectories requiring qualitatively different section
                               configurations.
                               Figs. S4–S6 plot the target positions and associated errors, but the
                               coordinates are not tabulated and the targets are not compared with
                               the complete reachable or sampled workspace. The authors should
                               report the target coordinates, associated bending and compression
                               ranges, and the fraction of the sampled workspace represented by
                               the evaluation set.
                               Each of the 20 targets is visited only once. Consequently, the
                               reported standard deviations describe variation across different
                               targets, not trial-to-trial repeatability, and do not establish that one
                               controller outperforms the other. The 30 waypoints in each trajectory
                               are likewise serially correlated rather than independent trials.
                               The disturbance experiment should also be characterized more
                               narrowly. A single 50 g off-center payload demonstrates
                               compensation for one constant load (which is on ly moderately
                               successful), not general robustness to disturbances or contact. Either
                               additional load magnitudes and directions should be tested or the
                               claims should be limited.


                               8. Establish independence of training and evaluation data
                               The datasets are randomly divided 85/10/5, but the acquisition
                               sequence, session structure, and grouping of neighboring
                               configurations are not described. If frames from continuous trials are
                               split randomly, nearly identical or temporally adjacent configurations
                               may appear in both training and test sets, inflating apparent
                               generalization.
                               The authors should state:
                               how samples were randomized;
                               whether test data were separated by session or configuration
                               neighborhood;
                               whether controller targets and trajectories were excluded from
                               training; and
                               whether experiments span different days, camera settings, coverings,
                               or robot builds.

                               9. Provide the missing reproducibility details
                               The paper needs to include enough information to enable
                               reproduction of the results. The methods omit several parameters
                               that affect performance: camera model, lens and field of view,
                               exposure and gain behavior, maximum epochs, random seeds,
                               output normalization statistics, angular decoding, and the training

                               Claims of robustness to lighting or hardware variation should be
                               limited unless such variation was explicitly tested.

                               Several figures—particularly Figs. 1B, 2, 3, and 5—are difficult to
