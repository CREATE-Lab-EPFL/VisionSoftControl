However, their virtually in#nite degrees of freedom and nonlinear deformations pose signi#cant challenges for sensing and control. This paper proposes a vision-based framework for distributed state estimation and closed-loop control of tendon-driven soft robotic arms. Miniature cameras embedded in each section provide dense proprioceptive feedback, while convolutional neural networks map multi-view images to both tip pose and full-body reconstruction using a polynomial curvature model. Two alternative controllers are designed and evaluated. The #rst is a model-based null-space velocity controller that regulates tip pose while shaping tendon con#gurations. The second is a data-driven inverse kinematics neural network that maps task-space goals to tendon commands, with explicit secondary-objective optimiza- tion during training. Both approaches are validated in open- and closed-loop settings on single- and multi-section manipulators in quasi-static regimes. Experiments demonstrate that embedded vision enables accurate full-body state reconstruction, reliable redundancy resolution, and robust regulation even under external disturbances. 1 Introduction Soft robots draw inspiration from natural organisms to achieve compliance and distributed motion [1]. They are designed to pas- sively deform and recon#gure to navigate complex environments and adapt to their surroundings. This embodied intelligence facil- itates compliant coupling with external objects, stemming from variable impedance characteristics [2] and potentially in#nite de- grees of freedom. Among soft robots, continuum manipulators are characterized by their slender con#guration, and capacity to have complex 3D curvatures. Due to their adaptability and safe interaction, they show promise in applications such as sur- gical tools for minimally invasive diagnosis [3], dexterous grasp- ing and manipulation [4], and environmental monitoring and exploration [5]. However, they still lack precision and robust- ness to external disturbances [6], requiring dense sensing and redundancy-aware control. Accurate proprioceptive control in soft arms depends on ade- quate feedback, as they are strongly a"ected by deformations induced by environmental interactions, self-weight, and approxi- mate modeling [7, 8]. Sensing technologies face major challenges, both in embedding sensors throughout the body and in recon- structing the end-e"ector pose from indirect measurements. One approach is to directly detect skin deformations using resistive or piezoresistive strain and pressure sensors, or Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG) nodes [5]. Another solution is to integrate Inertial Mea- surement Units (IMUs) into the arm, enabling high-frequency pose reconstruction while avoiding the occlusion and calibration issues of external sensing [6]. IMUs also allow estimation of body deformation under external forces [9]. However, IMU sensing su"ers from drift [10] and, being non- distributed, cannot capture the global strain state. Camera-based approaches are increasingly used in soft robotics for pose recon- struction, as they can capture both global deformations and local interactions [11], while avoiding the integration challenges of em- bedded strain or pressure sensors [5]. External setups have been proposed, including stereometric systems for supervised shape learning [12] and model-free approaches for closed-loop full-body control of soft robots [7]. These methods nevertheless become Copyright Statement © 2026 XXXX Advanced Intelligent Systems, Year;v0:1–13 https://doi.org/10.1002/0000 1 of 13 impractical in the presence of occlusions, and the cameras must remain #xed. For proprioceptive performance, the sensing system should track the robot motion from internal cameras, without interfering with movement or compliance of the structure. Previous works have embedded cameras inside the hollow body, often employing inter- nal illumination such as structured light [13] to ensure consistent imaging. In this framework, a common approach is to place visual markers beneath the robot skin. To further improve robustness, image binarization is often applied before inference [14], and sim- ulation is used to pre-train models for zero-shot transfer to the real world [15]. Approaches for reconstruction include geometric optimization from images [16] and deep learning architectures for state mapping. Embedded cameras provide high-resolution and accurate measurements of global deformations, without re- quiring complex distributed sensing hardware. Due to these ad- vantages, vision-based strategies have been exploited for contact and displacement detection [11], tactile servoing [17], and force estimation [18]. Onboard state estimation of soft structures allows for the deploy- ment of various control architectures. Yet, controlling continuum arms presents signi#cant challenges. These systems are inher- ently redundant [1] and therefore o"er multiple possibilities to con#gure their actuation space. They possess extra degrees of freedom beyond those required to accomplish the primary task, a property referred to as kinematic redundancy in the null space. This redundancy enables the optimization of secondary objec- tives, such as minimizing control e"ort, improving manipula- bility, and avoiding obstacles or singularities [19]. Prior works have applied null-space control of soft robots, employing path planning algorithms such as RRT [20] to randomize the robot con#guration while reaching a goal, or teleoperating the tip of a continuum manipulator with a rigid-link master to maximize manipulability [21]. More advanced objectives, such as whole- body grasping [22], have been demonstrated by assigning and regulating task-space coordinates at several locations along the backbone. However, validation of these approaches has been limited to simulation. Alternatively, researchers have controlled tendon-driven origami soft robots by implicitly solving redundant inverse kinematics through constrained gradient-based optimiza- tion, using motion capture for feedback [23]. Control strategies for continuum manipulators can be divided into model-based and data-driven approaches [1]. Many model- based controllers adopt a spatial backbone representation that neglects volumetric deformations and focuses exclusively on the behavior of the central axis [24]. A general kinematic formulation is the Piecewise Constant Curvature (PCC) model [25], which approximates the robot as a sequence of circular arcs. Variants of this model have also been developed for Jacobian-based con- trol [6]. More expressive alternatives include Cosserat rod models, which capture shear and torsional deformations through a ge- ometrically exact formulation [26]. FEM-based frameworks fur- ther leverage continuum mechanics for simulation and control, at the cost of higher computational demand [27]. Data-driven controllers address the limitations of these approaches, including non-idealities in soft robotics such as friction, material anisotropy, and cable slack [1]. One direction is adaptive control, where an online learning loop updates model parameters from sensor feedback [28]. Another is iterative learning control, where the 2 of 13 robot improves its behavior by incorporating errors from previ- ous executions into the feedforward command while preserving its natural compliance [2]. Alternatively, machine learning ap- proaches can approximate the entire robot model. Researchers have developed a biLSTM-based framework for planning and control that relies only on motor encoder feedback [10]. Addressing the sensing and control challenges of continuum manipulators, this paper proposes a strategy for vision-based re- construction and control of soft robotic arms that scales with the number of segments. Information from miniature cameras em- bedded in the sections is fused within a single CNN to achieve full-body reconstruction. The reconstructed state is then used as feedback in both model-based and data-driven controllers, en- abling tip regulation while reducing actuation e"ort. To the best of our knowledge, no framework has yet combined such sensing with closed-loop null-space control of continuum robots, either through model-based or learning-based approaches. The contributions of this paper include: 1. A distributed and scalable vision-based sensing strategy for soft arms that uses miniature cameras embedded in each section of the robot. It provides dense, markerless feedback for full-body state reconstruction, and scales to arms with di"erent numbers of sections through modular hardware design and a lightweight CNN architecture. 2. Integration of embedded vision with model-based null- space control. We close the loop by combining camera-based state estimation with a Jacobian-based Piecewise Constant Curvature velocity controller. 3. A data-driven framework for both sensing and control. We train learning-based inverse kinematics models to generate actuation commands while optimizing redundancy objec- tives. 4. Experimental validation of precise pose regulation using both model-based and data-driven controllers, and demon- stration of the advantages of closed-loop control under exter- nal disturbances. In the remainder of this paper, Section 2 describes the robot setup, the curvature models, and the data acquisition strategy. Section 3 presents the deep learning methods and the control architectures. Section 4 outlines the experimental results. Section 5 summarizes the paper and discusses potential directions for future work. Miniature cameras embedded in each segment feed deep learning algo- rithms that reconstruct the full shape of the robot, enabling closed-loop control strategies, both model-based and data-driven, which explicitly exploit kinematic redundancy to accomplish secondary tasks within the null space. Utilizing two tendon-driven continuum manipulators, we pro- pose a minimally invasive and compliance-preserving design us- ing a !exible opaque covering that scales along the arm length without a"ecting its behavior. Experimental results demonstrate that our sensing approach reaches competitive performance in reconstructing both tip pose and full-body curvature, with pre- served accuracy at workspace boundaries. Closed-loop control achieves near-sensing performance and outperforms open-loop alternatives, while being robust to external disturbances. This sensing and control framework endows soft robots with ac- curate motion capabilities. Leveraging their redundancy, contin- uum manipulators can accomplish complex tasks across multiple domains. For instance, they can be employed in search-and- rescue or inspection within con#ned and cluttered environments, or in minimally invasive surgery. In such settings, full-body curva- ture feedback from embedded cameras enables null-space control to actively shape the arm for obstacle avoidance while maintain- ing the desired tip pose. This strategy has some limitations, which may be addressed in future work. At the sensing level, the embedded vision system currently relies on ambient lighting provided by the external environment. Illumination passes through the opaque covering, and variability is mitigated through image augmentations during training. To enable operation in poorly lit or nocturnal environ- ments, the system should be extended with miniature internal light sources. This paper primarily investigates vision alone, inte- grating encoder signals for Jacobian estimation. Unlike external cameras, the internal setup avoids occlusion and #xed-placement constraints, at the cost of lower update frequency. The 8 Hz up- date rate limits the framework to quasi-static operation, suitable for applications such as inspection or surgical guidance; higher rates could be achieved by fusing camera estimates with encoder feedback through temporal #ltering. Future work should inte- grate the camera-based solution with alternative sensing strate- gies to improve con#guration accuracy. Moreover, the system does not detect contact or force information, although cameras could provide both. Extending proprioception to capture surface interactions through training or simulation would enhance robot capability, while force detection would add a feedback channel for closed-loop control in dynamic conditions. On the control side, future work will extend null-space control to additional sec- ondary objectives. In this paper, redundancy was exploited to balance tendon e"ort, but the same methodology applies to shap- ing arm con#gurations for obstacle avoidance while tracking a desired end-e"ector trajectory. Furthermore, null-space control could be leveraged to wrap around objects and enable grasping, using curvature feedback to regulate how the robot conforms to complex geometries.
