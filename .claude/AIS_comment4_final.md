## Letter block (replaces the Comment 4 block in `response_letter.tex`)

```latex
\begin{point}[Strengthen the ground truth for full-body reconstruction]\label{c:4}
For the single-section arm, curvature labels are fitted from three measured orientations. For the multi-section arm, SI Section 4.2 states that marker occlusion prevents simultaneous full-body tracking and that a learned network, $N_C$, maps tip pose to curvature labels. Thus, the multi-section CNN is evaluated primarily against a learned, model-constrained surrogate rather than direct measurements of the backbone.

Fig.~S2b does not provide sufficient validation of $N_C$: numerical errors, sample count, data split, and confidence intervals are missing. It is also unclear whether its validation configurations are independent of the data used to train $N_C$ and the three-section curvature estimator.

Wherever possible, reconstructed backbone points should be compared directly with motion-capture marker positions, and uncertainty in $N_C$ should be propagated into the reported errors. Otherwise, the result should be described as model-constrained reconstruction, with its assumptions stated explicitly, including planar bending within each section, no torsion, and no arc-dependent bending direction.
\end{point}
\begin{reply}
We thank the reviewer for this comment and agree that the multi-section curvature reference is a learned, model-constrained surrogate rather than a direct measurement of the backbone. Marker occlusion prevents full-body tracking of the multi-section arm during data collection, and a direct comparison with marker positions would require a new capture session with an occlusion-avoiding marker layout, which is outside the scope of this revision. We therefore describe the multi-section curvature results as model-constrained reconstruction in the main text and state the assumptions of the curvature model explicitly: planar bending within each section, no torsion, and a constant bending direction within each section. The Supporting Information now corrects the description of the surrogate, which maps the tip pose and tendon lengths of each module to the curvature parameters, and reports its validation numerically, including the number of held-out configurations, the data split and the type of interval. The surrogate was trained and validated on single-section data only, which are disjoint from the multi-section data used to train the multi-section estimator. Its error is small for the constant and affine models but not negligible for the quadratic model relative to the reconstruction error, which we now state; we do not propagate it analytically but report it alongside the reconstruction errors.
\end{reply}
```
